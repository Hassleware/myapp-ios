# Dream Table: notes

Table tennis between two wooden mannequins on a cloud island: you against a polite robot that can't lose.
Fantasy rules on top of real ones: sky balls you play in a jump, wide balls you dash for, a hover, a jet under
each torso. Portrait only (`game.cfg`; design and check every screen upright, iPhone frame `1u`). Runs on a
PS1's budget (`budget.lua`). This file is the map; the code's own comments explain each piece.

## Where things are

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `main.lua` | The tuning tables, the shared `game` table, the mannequins' bodies (`new_mannequin`, `move_body`: walking, the charted jump, hover, dash; `pose_mannequin`: flutter, lean, retro-burn, hidden legs, serving arm), your touches (`read_touches`, `start_swipe`, `whiff`), and the engine callbacks `init`, `fixed_update`, `update`, `draw` |
| `vec.lua` | Vector and quaternion helpers. A *turn* is a quaternion `{ x, y, z, w }` |
| `skeleton.lua` | The bones: reading and setting turns, mirroring and blending poses, where the bat's blade is, the flat serving bat and lifted arm, and `load_moves` (every recorded swing, and its mirror image) |
| `planner.lua` | `plan_swing`: which move, where to be and when to start it so the bat meets the ball. `plan_robot_swing`, `plan_your_swing` and the hints (`start_hint`) all use it. Plans are coroutines spread over fixed steps (`PLAN_SLICE`) |
| `rally.lua` | The ball (`step_ball`: flight, net, bounces, sidespin), aiming (`velocity_to_land`, `serve_shot`), hits (`launch_ball`, `your_hit`, `robot_hit`), points, serving turns, new games |
| `serve_meter.lua` | Your serve's capsule meter, the falling star, the Perfect / Good / Okay / Fail rating |
| `view.lua` | `draw_world` (3D) and `draw_ui` (2D), the camera (`CAM`), the ball's trail, messages (`show_message`) |
| `jump_chart.lua` | The hand-charted jump: 90 heights, one a frame, and the tables worked out from them |
| `scenery.lua` | The backdrop dome, the island and the cloud cards (made by `tools/blender_dreamtable_world.py`) |
| `budget.lua` | The PS1 budget: limits, the worst seen, a meter (B on the PC), `budget.report()` |
| `bitfont.lua` + `bitfont_metrics.lua` + `font_*.png` | Text in your dot font, baked with its outline (`tools/dream-table/make_bitmap_font.py`) |
| `lib.lua` | The template's shared helpers (identical to `tools/template/lib.lua`) |
| `shaders/backdrop.slang`, `shaders/trail.slang` | The unlit sky dome; the ball's ribbon trail |

**How the files connect.** Each module is `include("x.lua")(game)` and lists what it uses at its top, grouped by
the file it comes from. State that more than one file changes lives on the shared `game` table: `game.state`
(`"serve"`, `"toss"`, `"rally"`, `"point"`, `"over"`), `game.server`, `game.last_hitter`, `game.bounces`,
`game.score`, `game.timer`, `game.hint`, `game.wins`. Sides are always `"you"` and `"robot"`, and the mannequin
objects are `you` and `robot`. `main.lua` creates them in this order: vec, skeleton, (the mannequins), serve_meter,
planner, view, rally. A module that needs one made after it looks it up when called (`game.serve_turn`).

**Words.** A *move* is one playable recorded swing (a clip, maybe mirrored). A *clip* is the animation data. A
*stroke* is forehand or backhand. A *swing* is a move being played toward contact. A mannequin `m` is the whole
player; its *body* `m.body` (`b`) is its place and motion. The *jet* (`b.jet`: flare, hover) is the flame under
the torso. The *robot* is the far mannequin (never "CPU").

## Tuning

Every number to tune is in a table at the top of `main.lua`, with a comment saying what it does: `SERVE`, `DASH`
(including the retro-burn), `JET` (flame and hover), `LEAN` (lean and fall flutter), `FANTASY` (sky and wide
balls), `REACH` (how close the bat must come), `CUE` (hint timing), `FEEL` (hit-stop, shake, sounds), and in
`view.lua` `CAM`. Group new constants into tables: a Lua chunk may have at most 200 locals.

## Testing

- Run it hidden and fast. Append a probe to `main.lua` and run with `GAMELAB_TURBO=60 GAMELAB_NO_MSAA=1
  GAMELAB_HIDDEN=1 GAMELAB_DEVICE=1u GAMELAB_SHOT=... GAMELAB_SHOT_FRAME=...`. Count progress in fixed steps,
  and restart with `game.rally`'s `new_game()` when `game.state == "over"`. Override `sys.time_scale` (or set
  `FEEL.stop = 0`) so the hit-stop doesn't slow it down, and never let a probe call `save.set` (it would change
  your saved wins).
- A behaviour check that survives refactors: a probe that only touches the engine (`input.touches` fed with fake
  swipes and flicks, `sys.real_time` stepped with the game) and reads `ball` and `game.score`, with a fixed
  random seed, gives the same trace hash every run. The CEV refactor of 21 November 2026 was checked this way:
  3781b6b0 (swipes) and 199a66a6 (swipes, jumps, dashes, hovers) before and after every step.
- `python tools/dev.py check dream-table` after any change to 2D drawing or touch controls.
- `lua budget.report()` from dev.py, or B on the PC, for the PS1 budget.

- **Dream Table is portrait only** (`orientation = portrait` in its `game.cfg`, decided by the user on 16 November 2026: design and check every Dream Table screen upright, frame 1u). A line drawn before the robot has hit waits (`pending`). On your serve the line tosses the ball off your bat. Tested with random lines: 23 of 24 returned, several stroke types chosen. Feet (16 November 2026, after "it just slides around"): the recordings hold one ankle 6-9 cm up, so the body drops (`body.drop`) until its lowest foot is on the floor, a foot counts as the animation's own step only when `LIFTED` (7 cm) above the other, planted feet sit flat, the body's speed is capped (`TOP_SPEED` 2.2 m/s), steps alternate and may overlap halfway (0.16 s), a foot two strides behind steps anyway, and a foot sits exactly on its target (only mode switches fade): frames with a planted foot gliding went from about 25% to 0.5-3%. On the left half, a drag walks; letting go while the finger still moves faster than `FLICK_SPEED` (measured over its last 0.1 s) jumps that way, continuous in angle (all 360 degrees) and power: the more sideways, the lower, longer and more tipped over (up to `DIVE_LEAN`), up toward the table, down back. Past `DOWN_FROM` (sideways squared times power) it stays down after landing, up to `DOWN_MOST` 1.4 s, unable to walk or jump, then gets up slowly. - **Dream Table runs on a PS1's budget** (17 November 2026, the user's constraint: "constraints invoke creativity"; a light PS1 touch only, not a full emulation). `sys.budget()` (engine, new) measures triangles drawn last frame (shadow and every pass included), Lua instructions last frame (a count hook every 100; `lua_ops_now` for timing a piece of code), the game's own texels and largest texture (fonts count only glyphs drawn), mesh vertices and triangles, animation bytes at 16 bits a number, and the Lua heap. `games/dream-table/budget.lua` holds the limits (4000 triangles, 512K texels and none over 256, 2 MB RAM, 50000 Lua instructions a frame; the first 60 frames are loading and not counted), keeps the worst, draws a meter (B on the PC) and prints `budget.report()` (also `lua budget.report()` from dev.py). Measured in a self-played rally: worst 2580 triangles, 292K texels, 748 KB, 39900 instructions (it started at 450,000 triangles, 2.65M texels, 7 MB steady and 94 MB while building, 108,700 instructions). How: everything far away is rendered once in Blender (`tools/blender_dreamtable_world.py backdrop`: Cycles volumetric cumulus, beanstalks, cloud sea, gradient sky, from the camera's resting place, a 110 x 85 degree equirectangular slice; calibrated: yaw linear across, pitch linear down) into two 256 textures on a 96-triangle dome slice (`shaders/backdrop.slang`, no light or fog); the island (`island`) is a 32-spoke radial mesh, flat in the middle with a scalloped lip, sunlight and skylight baked into vertex colours (soft sun) and drawn unlit; `sprite` makes a cloud card. The pebbles and vines were removed on request (17 November 2026); the table stands on bare cloud. The mannequin and bats went through `tools/dream-table/blender_ps1_diet.py` (decimate with weights and animations kept, textures to 128 and 64, normal maps unplugged): 974 to 387 and 9872 to 98 triangles. The shadow map is off: soft blob shadows instead (a 32 x 32 round soft texture on a 2-triangle quad, under the table, each body, each planted foot and the ball). The ball is a 60-triangle mesh, sparks are particles, swipe dots are squares. Text is a bitmap font (`bitfont.lua`): `tools/dream-table/make_bitmap_font.py <ttf> <folder> [px]` bakes MyDotFont at 30 px with a smooth anti-aliased outline (0.11 of the size) and a soft drop shadow into `font_1.png`, `font_2.png` (256 each, 94 characters) and `font.lua` (metrics by character code); each letter is one `gfx.image`, white inside so the colour tints it and the outline stays dark. The run-time outline (copies of the words nudged about) was jagged at the corners. The .ttf stays in the folder as the source. Move planning is the one big piece of Lua: blade offsets once per move, flight points every 1/20 s between 0.3 and 2.2 m high, serve moves filtered by hand first, and each plan is a coroutine spread over steps (`PLAN_SLICE` 8 moves a fixed step, contact time corrected by the wait); self-play still returns 21 of 21 with the bat. - **Dream Table's opponent is the mannequin** playing shots-2's 11 shots, each also mirrored at run time (left-hand bat, `mirror_pose`: every bone takes its partner's turn-from-rest reflected across x, hips position flipped), so 22 moves (`games/dream-table/mannequin.glb`, built with `blender_mannequin.py -- <many .npz> <out.glb>`). The split finds each real hit as the bat wrist's fastest moment near its beep (about 0.8 s after it on the phone), clips are hit-centred (`CONTACT` = 2.0 s), and the guess (swing across the body: right to left forehand) matched watching the video on 10 of 11. When you hit, `plan()` searches the ball's predicted flight (after its bounce on the far side, off the table's end) for the point and move that fit best: blade height, how far to walk, how often the move was used (variety), with up to `CROUCH` 0.3 m of knee bend; it starts the clip so contact lands on time and walks there. The ball is returned only if it is within `HIT_RANGE` of the real blade (blade offsets from `blender_bats.py`): 24 returns, 0 misses, all 22 moves and all 9 shot types in a long PC test. Feet: planted, stepping one at a time when 0.16 m behind (arc 0.07 m, 0.2 s), the animation's own steps kept, two-bone IK. Ball: lit, a round shadow always straight below on table or floor, a burst where bat meets ball. Switching moves keeps the old pose and blends every bone into the new one over `BLEND_TIME` 0.18 s (works across mirrored and unmirrored; `write_pose`), the facing eases, foot targets ease, and knees are pulled toward the body's facing so they can't flip: biggest bone turn per 1/60 s 207 to 36 degrees. Its serve: the ball rests on top of a bat, `TOSS` flicks it up, `plan(hand)` hits it on the way down with that hand (alternating R and L each serve); a dropped toss re-serves. It is a **polite robot that can't lose**: every return and serve is a gentle ball (0.8 s flight) landing mid-table on the player's half on their paddle's side; if its bat ever misses, a rescue returns the ball before it falls below 0.35 m or bounces twice on its side. Tested: 53 returns in one rally with no rescues needed; with bat contact disabled, 16 rescues and no points lost. The ball's trail is a ribbon drawn by `games/dream-table/shaders/trail.slang`: a fixed 40-segment strip (built once with `gfx.new_mesh`, so no mesh churn) bent in the vertex shader along a Catmull-Rom curve through six points (the ball now and every 1/30 s before), sent through `gfx.frame_param` 1-4 and `gfx.param` 1-2, camera-facing, tapered, faded, additive. `lib.animation`'s `play` takes `time`.
